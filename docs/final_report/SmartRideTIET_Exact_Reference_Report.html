ABSTRACT

SmartRideTIET is a modern web application created to improve intra-campus mobility and personal convenience during daily travel across Thapar Institute of Engineering and Technology (TIET). It provides users with a reliable platform where they can access verified campus e-rickshaws and receive timely assistance during urgent transitions. In addition, the system aims to reduce the delays and friction people face while walking alone or waiting at physical stands, especially between distant hostels and academic venues. By integrating real-time telemetry, calibrated 2D landmark coordinates, and smart decision-making, the application helps students and faculty choose campus transit that is not only faster but also affordable and safe.

The app allows students to register, view active campus rickshaws, and start an on-demand booking whenever they need to commute across the 250-acre perimeter. Moreover, techniques such as calibrated landmark-to-landmark tracking and automated dispatch queuing ensure that an available rickshaw can reach the passenger whenever required. When a ride is booked, the application instantly verifies the trip with a 4-digit security One-Time Password (OTP), thereby preventing unauthorized boarding and improving reliability. This seamless communication builds a sense of trust and accountability.

Furthermore, SmartRideTIET focuses on user experience by providing a clean interface that is easy to navigate across mobile devices. It connects essential micro-mobility functionalities—landmark search, live radar monitoring, cashless virtual wallet settlement backed by Razorpay, and administrative fleet oversight—into one unified system. Through these features, the app not only enhances daily convenience but also supports users in making informed decisions during travel. Ultimately, we conclude that SmartRideTIET offers a practical yet effective solution for students and campus drivers seeking enhanced efficiency, safety, and confidence in everyday campus mobility.

CHAPTER -1

INTRODUCTION

SmartRideTIET is a web-based mobile application designed to make campus travel easier, especially for students, faculty, and campus visitors at Thapar Institute of Engineering and Technology (TIET). The app goes beyond ordinary navigation by not only showing campus pathways, but also by connecting riders directly with verified campus e-rickshaws and optimizing intra-campus micro-mobility. It does this by combining campus GPS coordinates, landmark mapping, and machine learning analytics.

The system works through three main parts:

  • A mobile web app (React.js, Vite) where users can explore 60+ campus landmarks, request rides, view trip fares, and track drivers.
  • A backend orchestration service (Node.js, Express) that manages role-based authentication, ride state transitions, wallet transactions, and administrative oversight.
  • A machine learning service (FastAPI, Python) that analyzes campus ride demand, forecasts revenue, evaluates cancellation risk, and computes real-time surge multipliers.

SmartRideTIET brings in data from multiple sources. It uses calibrated 2D campus layout maps to select origins and destinations, and Google Maps API to display real-time coordinates. Campus transit corridors are identified using landmark clustering, and each ride is assigned an equitable flat fare of ₹10 for standard intra-campus hops. In addition, an analytical engine monitors hourly fleet demand, ensuring that drivers are positioned effectively across peak hostel-to-class corridors.

What makes SmartRideTIET unique is its focus on real-time safety and convenience:

  • Users receive 4-digit security OTP check-ins during boarding, confirming driver identity.
  • If assistance is required, the app provides direct emergency alerts connecting to TIET Main Security Gate and campus medical helplines.
  • Push notifications and live state updates are handled through Firebase Firestore listeners, keeping the system responsive and synchronized.

Security and privacy are built in at every level. User data, including travel history and emergency contacts, is protected with secure cryptographic tokens, and the system is designed to meet university compliance and privacy standards. In simple terms, SmartRideTIET isn't just another booking app. It's an intelligent campus companion that helps people move around TIET with more confidence, turning complex transit coordination into clear, easy-to-use micro-mobility insights.

1.2 Problem Statement

The problem statement revolves around the need for an intelligent micro-mobility and dispatch application that provides reliable intra-campus travel for students and staff navigating expansive university grounds. While conventional navigation systems focus primarily on public municipal roads, they ignore the unique layout of closed academic campuses, leaving students stranded during urgent class transitions or late night hours.

The primary challenges addressed by our project include:

  • Campus-Aware Routing: Current navigation systems lack the ability to route vehicles through closed pedestrian campus pathways. There is a need for a system that integrates calibrated campus landmarks, spatial analysis, and dispatching to provide students with convenient intra-campus rides instead of external detours.
  • Real-Time Transit Awareness: Students often remain unaware of rickshaw availability at distant stands or hostel gates. A solution is required that updates active vehicle locations dynamically and alerts users about expected wait times based on hour of day and campus traffic patterns.
  • User-Friendly Interface: Existing commercial hailing apps are overly complex and economically impractical for short campus trips. Our project emphasizes an intuitive, user-friendly interface designed specifically for university students and rickshaw drivers, enabling rapid landmark selection and one-click booking.
  • Emergency Response Features: Traditional rickshaw hailing provides zero security records. There is a clear need for built-in safety tools such as 4-digit security OTPs, campus emergency helpline directories, and direct connection with TIET security control posts.
  • Data Privacy and Security: Since the system handles sensitive student profiles and payment records, it must comply with recognized standards for authentication, data encryption, and role-based segregation.

By addressing these challenges, the SmartRideTIET project aims to deliver a comprehensive solution that combines intra-campus navigation, dispatch intelligence, and institutional accountability. This ensures that students and faculty can commute across the university grounds with greater convenience, punctuality, and security.

In today's expansive higher education institutions, students and faculty are increasingly exposed to transit bottlenecks while commuting across large university campuses. The continuous expansion of instructional departments, specialized research blocks, and residential hostels across hundreds of acres has created an urgent need for transit solutions that go beyond traditional unorganized rickshaw hailing. 2.1 Theory Associated with Problem Area

Campus micro-mobility and automated dispatch systems are supported by several core theoretical foundations, and understanding these concepts is essential for developing an institutional application like SmartRideTIET. To begin with, the field of spatial landmark modeling and coordinate indexing provides the base for identifying pickup and destination points within a closed perimeter. Techniques such as Euclidean distance computation and clustering methods like DBSCAN help in grouping boarding points, and therefore, they play a key role in organizing transit corridors for users.

In addition to spatial modeling, the development of a Demand and Surge model is based on theories from time-series forecasting, regression analysis, and economic queuing theory. Here, various factors such as historical ride volume, hour of day, and calendar variables like weekends are combined. Furthermore, machine learning methods can be applied to learn from past trends, enabling the system to predict demand levels and cancellation risks more accurately.

Moving forward, the theory of route and dispatch optimization also contributes significantly. Traditional navigation depends on shortest-path algorithms such as Dijkstra’s or A*. However, SmartRideTIET adapts these techniques for institutional environments by using landmark-based matching, where the system balances vehicle proximity with driver availability. As a result, students receive recommendations that are not only quick but also practical within campus pedestrian boundaries.

Another important foundation comes from real-time communication and state synchronization systems. Concepts like event-driven architecture, reactive document snapshots, and WebSocket/Firestore listeners help ensure that ride requests reach available drivers without delay. Techniques such as atomic transactions and concurrency locks additionally help the system function reliably, preventing multiple drivers from accepting the same booking simultaneously.

Finally, principles of human–computer interaction (HCI) guide the design of a simple and accessible user interface. Since users may interact with the app during rushed class changes, theories related to usability, clarity, and minimal cognitive load become important. Meanwhile, secure virtual wallet management theory is required to store student balances, transaction history, and payment credentials while still supporting real-time performance.

By connecting these theoretical foundations—spatial landmark mapping, predictive modeling, dispatch queuing, real-time synchronization, and user-centered design—the SmartRideTIET application is able to create a campus transit system that is not only accurate and fast but also focused on student affordability. In the end, we can conclude that these combined theories form the backbone of SmartRideTIET and ensure that the system supports smooth and reliable university travel.

2.2 Existing Systems and Solutions

In the domain of transportation and ride dispatching, multiple existing systems and solutions have been developed to address mobility, vehicle tracking, and passenger booking. Understanding these existing tools helps in identifying their strengths and limitations, which the SmartRideTIET project aims to overcome.

  • Commercial Navigation & Ride Platforms: Popular applications such as Google Maps, Uber, and Ola provide efficient route computation and automated vehicle hailing based on distance and city traffic. However, these platforms typically ignore private university campuses, restrict commercial drivers at institutional gates, and enforce high minimum fares (₹50–₹100). As a result, students cannot utilize these services for short 800-meter intra-campus commutes.
  • Dockless Micro-Mobility Services: Several mobile systems, like Yulu and Lime, attempt to address short-distance travel through app-based bicycle or scooter sharing. While these platforms offer useful on-demand options, they require high maintenance, are vulnerable to equipment damage, and present physical accessibility challenges during adverse weather or for students carrying heavy equipment.
  • Fixed Campus Shuttle Bus Services: Universities often operate scheduled internal buses circling perimeter roads. Although they provide affordable transit, they operate on rigid schedules and fixed bus stops, failing to provide on-demand, point-to-point transit between interior academic blocks and distant hostel cul-de-sacs.
  • Informal Campus Rickshaw Stands: Many Indian institutions rely on manual e-rickshaws stationed at primary gates. These operators typically negotiate unmetered fares and cluster exclusively at high-visibility gates, leaving interior campus zones unserviced and offering zero digital record-keeping or safety oversight.
  • Academic Micro-Mobility Prototypes: Some research efforts have explored deploying automated dispatch algorithms for campus vehicles. While promising, many such prototypes remain confined to theoretical simulations and are not deployed as functional, accessible mobile web applications for university communities.

Despite the availability of these systems, there remains a clear gap in delivering a comprehensive, real-time, and user-friendly campus transit application that integrates landmark coordinate mapping, flat-rate student pricing, and automated dispatching. Existing solutions either focus solely on municipal roads or on rigid shuttle routes without combining both into a seamless campus companion.

The SmartRideTIET app addresses this gap by merging landmark indexing, machine learning–based demand prediction, virtual wallet payments, and safety verification into one unified platform, specifically designed to enhance daily mobility for students and staff within TIET.

CHAPTER-3

METHODOLOGY

The development of SmartRideTIET followed a structured methodology that blends software engineering, spatial data mapping, machine learning, and user-centered design. The goal was not just to create another navigation app, but to build a system that understands campus mobility challenges, particularly for university students and rickshaw drivers, and provides them with convenience and reliable travel.

The methodology can be described in the following stages:

3.1 Data Collection and Preprocessing

Every operational decision made by SmartRideTIET is based on real campus transit data. Without reliable spatial and telemetry records, the system cannot truly coordinate rides between different university sectors.

  • Sources: We surveyed and cataloged 60+ verified landmarks across the TIET Patiala campus (e.g., Hostels A–Q, Academic Blocks, Central Library, Sports Complex, Food Street, Main Entrance). Each landmark record included coordinates (latitude & longitude), campus category, and descriptive markers.
  • Preprocessing:
    • Calibrated raw GPS coordinates against high-resolution campus maps to eliminate boundary drift.
    • Categorized landmarks into functional sectors (Gates, Academic, Hostels, Sports, Food, Facilities) to facilitate dropdown searches.
    • Normalized ride transaction timestamps into hourly bins to train predictive machine learning models.
  • Storage: Structured landmark and user datasets were stored in Firebase Firestore, making them scalable, fast, and real-time synchronized.

3.2 Route Generation

Once the user selects a campus pickup landmark and destination, the system must propose the journey details.

  • We mapped campus arterial roads and pedestrian corridors connecting all 60+ landmarks.
  • These included direct walking paths, lighted central spines, and perimeter vehicle roads.
  • The output was a calibrated distance and fare quotation (flat ₹10 for intra-campus trips) displayed immediately on the user interface.

3.3 Crime Analysis and Hotspot Detection (Adapted: High-Demand Hub Detection)

Merely cataloging campus landmarks isn't enough. The app must analyze spatial demand patterns.

  • Proximity Analysis: For each ride request, we identified active e-rickshaws within campus geofence boundaries using spatial coordinate matching.
15
  • Hotspot Detection: Using landmark density clustering, we grouped high-volume boarding locations (such as the Central Library at 10:00 PM and COS Block at 1:00 PM). These clusters were marked as high-demand transit hubs.

3.4 Feature Extraction

To evaluate the dispatch feasibility and pricing of each ride, the system needed measurable factors.

For each ride and time slot, we extracted:

  • Ride Demand Count: How many ride requests occurred in the target hour?
  • Active Fleet Capacity: How many verified e-rickshaws were currently online?
  • Landmark Proximity: How close is the nearest available vehicle to the student?
  • Time of Day Multiplier: A time factor reflecting peak lecture transition windows (e.g., 8:00 AM, 1:00 PM, 5:00 PM).

3.5 Safety Score Prediction (Machine Learning) (Adapted: Predictive Analytics Engine)

The analytical intelligence of the system is powered by predictive regression and classification models in FastAPI:

  • Input Features: Day of week, weekend indicator, historical lag-1 rides, hour of day.
  • Output: Next-day demand estimate, 7-day revenue projection, and cancellation probability.
  • Final Score: A balance between base demand and capacity ratios to calculate dynamic surge multipliers (1.0× to 2.5×).

3.6 Route Ranking and Recommendation (Adapted: Dispatch & Matching Engine)

After a booking is initiated:

  • The ride request is broadcast to all active, verified drivers within the campus geofence.
  • The first driver to confirm claims the ride via atomic database transactions.
  • Students receive driver details, vehicle registration, and a 4-digit security OTP.

3.7 Emergency and Safety Features

Safety isn't just about ride tracking; it's also about mutual verification and response in real time.

  • Check-ins (4-Digit OTP): The app requires the driver to input the student's 4-digit security OTP before starting the trip.
  • Emergency SOS: If an emergency occurs, users can trigger an instant SOS alert displaying TIET Main Security Gate and Ambulance contact numbers.
  • Live Sharing: Students can share active trip status and vehicle details with friends or wardens.

MODEL DISCUSSION

5.1 Abstract

This document provides a detailed description of the machine learning (ML) technologies used in the SmartRideTIET model engine (`model.py`), designed to forecast campus ride demand, revenue, cancellation risk, and surge multipliers across TIET Patiala. The model integrates supervised learning (Linear Regression, Polynomial Regression, Logistic Regression), spatial landmark analysis, and dynamic heuristics to process campus transit logs and optimize fleet dispatch. Key formulas for demand forecasting, revenue modeling, and surge pricing calculations are presented, along with explanations of their roles and justifications for their selection. This document is intended to support an academic explanation of the model's methodology.

5.2 Introduction

The SmartRideTIET analytical model addresses the problem of coordinating intra-campus e-rickshaws across 60+ designated landmarks in TIET, prioritizing student punctuality and fair pricing over unregulated haggling. The model processes historical transit telemetry from Firebase Firestore with attributes RideID, PickupLandmark, DropLandmark, Timestamp, Status, Fare, and DriverID. It fetches active requests and evaluates them through a FastAPI server, forecasting tomorrow's demand and calculating real-time surge multipliers (1.0×–2.5×) based on temporal indicators (Morning, Afternoon, Evening, Night) and fleet capacity. The model combines rule-based flat fares (₹10 intra-campus) with machine learning to ensure robust, interpretable results. This document details the ML technologies used, including their mathematical formulations, roles, and justifications, to support an academic presentation. The technologies include Pandas, Scikit-learn, Linear Regression, Polynomial Features, Logistic Regression, and a FastAPI-based API.

5.3 Problem Definition

The objective is to optimize intra-campus transit between 60+ landmarks based on demand and fleet capacity, where service quality is determined by:

  • Wait Time Minimization: Rides matching nearby available rickshaws to reduce passenger wait time below 5 minutes.
  • Demand Patterns: Identifying high-traffic clusters (e.g., Central Library at closing, COS Block at lunch) to position idle vehicles proactively.
  • Predictable Pricing: Maintaining a flat ₹10 baseline fare for campus travel while applying a controlled surge cap during extreme rush periods.
26

Input:

  • Campus ride transaction logs with historical trip records.
  • Pickup and drop landmark coordinates (latitude, longitude).
  • Time category (Morning, Afternoon, Evening, Night).

Output: A JSON response with: predicted_rides_tomorrow, 7day_revenue_forecast, cancellation_risk_pct, and surge_multiplier (1.0–2.5×).

5.4 Working Of Machine Learning Model

1. Transit Data Preparation

  • A historical campus transit dataset is used containing:
    • Pickup Latitude, Longitude
    • Drop Latitude, Longitude
    • Trip Status (Completed / Cancelled)
    • Fare and Timestamp
  • Landmark coordinates are converted into numerical format for spatial computation.
  • Maximum observed daily demand in the dataset is computed as:
\( D_{max} = \max(d_i) \)

2. Spatial Indexing of Landmarks

  • A KDTree / Geofence index is constructed using landmark latitude and longitude values.
  • This allows fast identification of nearby vehicles and landmarks.
  • Distance between two campus points is computed as:
\( d(p,q) = \sqrt{(lat_p - lat_q)^2 + (lon_p - lon_q)^2} \)

3. Demand Hotspot Detection

  • DBSCAN clustering is applied to detect dense student boarding zones (hotspots).
  • Parameters used:
    • \( arepsilon = 0.001\) (≈ 100 meters)
    • Minimum samples = 5
  • For each detected cluster:
    • Centroid:
27
\( C_k = \left( rac{1}{n_k} \sum lat_i, \; rac{1}{n_k} \sum lon_i ight) \)
  • Average passenger volume:
\( ar{V}_k = rac{1}{n_k} \sum v_i \)
  • Clusters with high average passenger volume are marked as campus transit hubs.

4. Route Generation

  • Direct pathways are mapped between campus origin and destination landmarks.
  • Impassable pedestrian pathways and vehicular barriers are filtered out.
  • Only authorized campus e-rickshaw corridors are retained for transit.

5. Mapping Landmarks to Routes

  • Each transit path is divided into intermediate campus waypoints.
  • Nearby landmark points within fixed radii are indexed using spatial trees.
  • Duplicate trip records are resolved using unique Ride IDs.

6. Route Distance Calculation

  • Route distance is calculated using geodesic distance between consecutive waypoints:
\( D_{route} = \sum_{i=1}^{n-1} geodesic(p_i, p_{i+1}) \)

7. Feature Extraction for Each Route

For every operating period and route, the following features are computed:

  • Total number of completed rides
  • Average daily demand:
\( ar{D} = rac{\sum d_i}{N} \)
  • Maximum peak-hour demand
  • Number of peak rush rides:

\( d_i \ge 0.6 imes D_{max} \)
  • Route distance (km)
  • Day of the week index (0=Monday to 6=Sunday)
  • Weekend binary flag (1 for Saturday/Sunday, 0 for weekdays)
  • Historical lag-1 ride count (yesterday's total)
  • Active available rickshaw fleet count
  • Instantaneous demand-to-capacity ratio

These features together represent the operational profile of campus mobility.

8. Safety Score Calculation (Rule-Based) (Adapted: Dynamic Surge Calculation)

Process: Computes a surge multiplier to balance rider demand against available rickshaws during rush hours.

Formula: \( surge\_multiplier = \min(2.5, \; \max(1.0, \; 1.0 + \gamma imes ( ho - 1.0))) imes time_{multiplier} \)

  • Demand-supply ratio:
\( ho = rac{rides_{requested}}{\max(active_{rickshaws}, 1)} \)
  • Campus elasticity factor: \(\gamma = 0.75\)
  • Time multiplier:
\( time_{multiplier} = egin{cases} 1.2 & ( ext{Morning Rush 8-9am, Evening 5-7pm}) \ 1.1 & ( ext{Lunch Peak 12-1pm}) \ 1.0 & ( ext{Normal Hours}) \end{cases} \)

Normalization:

\( surge_{normalized} = \min(2.5, \; surge\_multiplier) \)

If \( surge\_multiplier > 1.0 \):

9. Machine Learning Model Training

  • Historical daily aggregations are generated from Firestore records.
  • Feature vectors \([day\_of\_week, is\_weekend, lag\_1]\) are standardized.
  • A Linear Regression model is trained using:
\( \hat{y} = heta_0 + \sum_{j=1}^{3} heta_j x_j \)

where parameters \(oldsymbol{ heta}\) minimize the mean squared error on training observations.

  • Final revenue projection is computed as:

using degree-2 polynomial basis expansions over operating days.

11. Route Ranking (Adapted: Driver Dispatch Allocation)

  • All candidate nearby e-rickshaws are evaluated in order of proximity.
  • The nearest available driver is notified and allocated the ride upon acceptance.

The SmartRideTIET model engine effectively combines spatial landmark indexing, demand clustering, and supervised learning to coordinate campus transit.
